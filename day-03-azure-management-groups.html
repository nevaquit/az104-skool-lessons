The concept

Management groups are containers for subscriptions. They exist for 1 reason: governance at scale. Instead of assigning the same policy or role 50 times across 50 subscriptions, you assign it once at a management group and everything underneath inherits it.

The hierarchy:

  1. Tenant Root Group: every Entra tenant has exactly 1. Every subscription lives under it unless you move it into a child management group.
  2. Child management groups: you create these, up to 6 levels deep (the root plus 5 levels below it).
  3. Subscriptions: sit under the root group or under any child group.
  4. Below subscriptions, the usual resource groups and resources.

2 rules the exam tests constantly:

  • Inheritance flows down. A policy or role assignment at a management group applies to every subscription, resource group, and resource beneath it. You cannot block inheritance for a child scope.
  • The management group ID is immutable. You set it at creation and can never change it. The display name can change, the ID cannot. Pick IDs carefully: lowercase, no spaces, something like mg-meridian-prod.

Practical limits worth knowing: up to 10,000 management groups per tenant. Moving a subscription between management groups is allowed and does not move or rename any resources inside it.

The classic enterprise pattern is the landing zone: a root management group for the company, child groups for production and non-production, and subscriptions under each. 1 "allowed regions" policy at the top controls every subscription below.

Key vocabulary

  • Management group: a container for subscriptions that lets you apply governance once and inherit it everywhere below.
  • Tenant Root Group: the top level management group every tenant has. All subscriptions start under it.
  • Inheritance: policies and role assignments flow down the hierarchy and cannot be blocked by a child scope.
  • Scope: the level where a policy or role assignment applies: management group, subscription, resource group, or resource.
  • Management group ID: the unique identifier set at creation. It can never be changed.

Try it

Creating a management group is free. In most tenants you can create 1; if the create option is disabled, you need the Management Group Contributor role on the tenant root group.

In the portal:

  1. Search for Management groups and open it. Study the hierarchy: Tenant Root Group at the top, your subscriptions beneath it.
  2. Click Create. Enter a management group ID you will never need to change, for example mg-lab-01, and a display name like Lab MG 01. Submit and watch it appear in the tree.

In the CLI (Cloud Shell is fine):

  1. List the hierarchy: az account management-group list --output table.
  2. Create 1: az account management-group create --name mg-lab-02 --display-name "Lab MG 02".
  3. Verify it shows up in the portal tree, then clean up: az account management-group delete --name mg-lab-02. Delete the portal 1 too from its Overview page.

Clean up: delete both lab management groups. Nothing billable was created.

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian Health outgrows a single subscription and builds a hierarchy:

  • Tenant Root Group
  • mg-meridian (display name "Meridian Health")
  • mg-meridian-prod → Meridian-Prod subscription
  • mg-meridian-devtest → Meridian-DevTest subscription
  • mg-meridian-security → Meridian-Security subscription

They assign an "allowed locations: East US only" policy at mg-meridian-prod. Every current and future subscription under it inherits the rule automatically. When a fourth subscription for a new clinic is added under mg-meridian-prod next quarter, the policy is already there. That is the whole point: govern once, inherit everywhere.

  2. You assign an Azure Policy at a management group. A new subscription is added under that group next month. Does the policy apply to it?
  3. True or false: you can change a management group ID after creation.
  4. How many levels deep can a management group hierarchy go?

Exam tip

Inheritance is the entire reason management groups exist on this exam. Any question about applying 1 rule to many subscriptions is answered at the management group scope. And the immutable ID is a favorite trap: if a scenario renames a group and asks what breaks, the answer is nothing, because the ID (what Azure actually uses) never changes.

Key takeaways

  • Management groups contain subscriptions and bring governance at scale through inheritance.
  • Every tenant has exactly 1 Tenant Root Group; hierarchies go 6 levels deep.
  • Policies and role assignments flow down and cannot be blocked by child scopes.
  • The management group ID is set at creation and can never change.
  • Moving a subscription between groups changes governance, not the resources inside.
