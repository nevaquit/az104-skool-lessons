The concept

Azure Policy is how you control what resources are allowed to exist in your environment. It answers a different question than RBAC. RBAC answers "who can do what." Policy answers "what is allowed to exist, and in what shape."

It works in 2 parts:

  1. A policy definition: the rule itself. For example: "storage accounts must not allow public blob access" or "virtual machines may only use approved SKUs."
  2. A policy assignment: applying that rule to a scope. Assignments can target a management group, a subscription, or a resource group.

Once assigned, Azure evaluates every new or changed resource against the policy. Resources that already exist are picked up by a periodic compliance scan. Anything that breaks the rule is reported on the compliance dashboard, and depending on the effect you chose, it is blocked or just flagged.

The effects you will see on the exam:

  • Deny: blocks the creation or update. The deployment fails with a policy error.
  • Audit: allows the resource but flags it as non-compliant.
  • AuditIfNotExists: audits when a related resource is missing (for example, no diagnostic settings on a VM).
  • DeployIfNotExists: deploys the missing piece automatically (for example, installs an agent).
  • Modify: adds or changes properties, such as adding a required tag.
  • Disabled: the definition is turned off.

1 line to lock in: policy never grants access. A user with the Owner role who hits a Deny policy is still blocked, because RBAC and Policy control 2 different things.

Key vocabulary

  • Policy definition: the rule, written in JSON. Defines the condition and the effect.
  • Assignment: applying a definition to a scope so it takes effect.
  • Scope: where a policy applies: management group, subscription, or resource group.
  • Effect: what happens on a violation: Deny, Audit, AuditIfNotExists, DeployIfNotExists, Modify, or Disabled.
  • Initiative: a bundle of policy definitions assigned as 1 unit (Day 14).
  • Compliance: the measured state of your resources against assigned policies.
  • Remediation: bringing non-compliant resources back into compliance (Day 14).

Try it

You need any Azure account, including a free 1. This lab is read only. Nothing is created or changed.

In the portal:

  1. Search for Policy and open it.
  2. Under Authoring, open Definitions. In the search box, type "Allowed locations" and open the built-in definition. Read the description and note the parameters it asks for.
  3. Go to Compliance in the left menu. This dashboard shows compliant versus non-compliant resources for every assignment. Open any assignment to see the breakdown.

In the CLI (Cloud Shell is fine):

  1. List built-in definitions matching a keyword: az policy definition list --query "[?contains(displayName,'Allowed locations')].{Display:displayName, Mode:mode}" --output table
  2. List current assignments in your subscription: az policy assignment list --query "[].{Name:name, Scope:scope}" --output table

Clean up: nothing was created or changed.

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian Health must keep patient data in 1 Azure region for compliance. The cloud team assigns the built-in "Allowed locations" policy with a Deny effect at the Meridian-Prod subscription scope.

A developer with the Contributor role tries to create a storage account in a different region. RBAC says yes: Contributors can create storage accounts. Policy says no: the location is not allowed. The deployment fails with a policy violation error. This is the exam pattern in real life: RBAC controls who acts, Policy controls what may exist, and neither 1 overrides the other.

Exam tip

Any question containing "prevent anyone from creating," "ensure all resources comply," or "block deployments that" points to Azure Policy, usually with a Deny effect. If the question is about who may manage or view resources, that is RBAC instead. The exam loves making you choose between the 2.

Key takeaways

  • Azure Policy controls what resources may exist; RBAC controls who may act.
  • A definition is the rule; an assignment applies it to a management group, subscription, or resource group.
  • New and changed resources are evaluated immediately; existing resources are caught by the compliance scan.
  • Know the effects: Deny blocks, Audit flags, DeployIfNotExists and Modify fix, Disabled turns off.
  • Policy never grants access, and a Deny policy stops even an Owner.
