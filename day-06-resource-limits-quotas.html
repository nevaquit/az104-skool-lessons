The concept

Azure does not let you consume infinite resources. Every subscription has default limits, and most are enforced per region. 2 kinds exist:

  • Adjustable quotas. These can be raised with a support request. The classic example is vCPU quotas per VM series per region: a new subscription might allow only 10 vCPUs of a given family in East US. Need 50? File a quota increase request.
  • Hard limits. These cannot be raised. Examples: 980 resource groups per subscription, 250 storage accounts per region per subscription, 50 tags per resource. Design around them.

Why quotas exist: they protect you from runaway deployments (and runaway bills) and protect the platform from any single tenant hogging capacity.

Where to look: in the portal, open Subscriptions, pick 1, and open Usage + quotas under Settings. Filter by region and provider to see current usage against the ceiling. In the CLI, az vm list-usage --location eastus shows VM related quotas.

3 facts the exam repeats:

  1. Quotas are per subscription per region. Hitting the vCPU ceiling in East US does not stop you from deploying in West Europe.
  2. New subscriptions start lower. Fresh or low trust subscriptions get smaller default quotas, especially for VM vCPUs. Quota increase requests on new subscriptions can take longer.
  3. The fixes for "quota exceeded" are always the same 3: deploy in a different region, deploy in a different subscription, or request a quota increase through Help + support (issue type: Service and subscription limits (quotas)).

Quotas are ceilings, not allowances. They never "reset" each month. If you use 8 of 10 allowed vCPUs, you have 2 left until you delete something or raise the quota.

Key vocabulary

  • Quota: an adjustable ceiling on a resource type, enforced per subscription per region.
  • (Usage) limit: a hard ceiling that cannot be raised.
  • Support request: the formal way to ask Microsoft to raise an adjustable quota: Help + support, issue type Service and subscription limits (quotas).
  • vCPU quota: the most commonly hit quota: how many virtual CPUs of a VM family you may run per region.

Try it

Read only. Nothing is created and nothing is billed.

In the portal:

  1. Search for Subscriptions, open yours, and select Usage + quotas under Settings.
  2. Set the region filter to East US (or your home region). Find Total Regional vCPUs and 1 VM family line such as Standard DSv5 Family vCPUs. Note the current usage versus the limit.
  3. Change the provider filter to Storage and find the storage account limit for the region.

In the CLI (Cloud Shell is fine):

  1. Show VM usage for a region: az vm list-usage --location eastus --output table.
  2. Find the line for total regional vCPUs and compare it with what the portal showed.

Clean up: nothing was created.

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian Health's prod scale-out fails on a Friday night: "QuotaExceeded: Standard Dv5 Family vCPUs in East US." The on-call admin does not panic. She knows the 3 fixes: the deployment can wait for a quota increase ticket, it can go to West US 2 where headroom exists, or a second subscription can take the load. She files the support request for a permanent raise and deploys the urgent VMs to West US 2 to unblock the release. Monday's retro adds "check quota headroom" to the pre-deployment checklist.

Exam tip

"Deployment failed with quota exceeded" has exactly 3 answers: different region, different subscription, support request for a quota increase. The exam also likes the new subscription twist: a recently created subscription fails where an older 1 succeeds, because default quotas start lower.

Key takeaways

  • Quotas are adjustable ceilings per subscription per region; hard limits cannot be raised.
  • vCPU quotas per VM family are the most commonly hit ceiling.
  • Check Usage + quotas in the portal or az vm list-usage in the CLI.
  • Fix quota errors with a new region, a new subscription, or a support request.
  • Quotas never reset; they are ceilings, not monthly allowances.
