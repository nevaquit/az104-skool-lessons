The concept

An Azure subscription is 2 things at once: a billing boundary and an access boundary. Every dollar of Azure spend rolls up to exactly 1 subscription, and every role assignment and policy you create lives inside a subscription unless you scope it higher.

The hierarchy, top to bottom:

  1. Microsoft Entra tenant: your organization's identity boundary. 1 tenant can hold many subscriptions.
  2. Management groups: containers for subscriptions. This is where governance at scale lives (Day 3).
  3. Subscriptions: the billing and access boundary.
  4. Resource groups: logical containers inside a subscription (Day 4).
  5. Resources: the actual VMs, storage accounts, and databases.

Why use more than 1 subscription? 3 reasons the exam loves:

  • Billing separation. Finance wants 1 invoice per department or per environment. Separate subscriptions give you that without any tagging gymnastics.
  • Access boundaries. You can make someone a Contributor on the dev subscription and give them nothing on production. That is cleaner than carving up 1 subscription with dozens of role assignments.
  • Limits and quotas. Quotas apply per subscription per region (Day 6). Splitting workloads across subscriptions raises your effective ceiling.

Common subscription types:

  • Free Trial: 30 days plus limited credits. Good for learning, expires.
  • Pay-As-You-Go: the default. Pay for what you use, cancel anytime.
  • Enterprise Agreement (EA) / Microsoft Customer Agreement (MCA): negotiated pricing for large organizations, with enrollments and departments under them.
  • Cloud Solution Provider (CSP): bought through a partner, common for small and mid-size businesses.

A subscription has a lifecycle too: Active, Disabled, Warned, Deleted. If payment fails, the subscription is disabled and resources are eventually deleted. On the exam, "the subscription was disabled for nonpayment" explains why resources vanished.

Key vocabulary

  • Subscription: the billing and access boundary in Azure. All usage is metered against 1 subscription.
  • Tenant: the Microsoft Entra ID instance for your organization. Holds users, groups, and 1 or more subscriptions.
  • Management group: a container above subscriptions for governance at scale.
  • Quota (limit): the maximum of a resource type allowed per subscription per region. Adjustable by support request.
  • Offer: the pricing and terms plan attached to a subscription (for example, Pay-As-You-Go, Free Trial, EA).

Try it

You need any Azure account, including a free 1. Nothing here creates billable resources.

In the portal:

  1. Search for Subscriptions and open it. Note each subscription's name, subscription ID, and current cost view.
  2. Click 1 subscription, then open Resource providers under Settings. Notice which providers are Registered. A resource type cannot be created until its provider is registered in that subscription.
  3. Open Cost Management from the subscription menu. Look at the accumulated cost view by resource. This is the billing boundary in action: every charge sits under exactly 1 subscription.

In the CLI (Cloud Shell is fine):

  1. List your subscriptions: az account list --output table. Note the IsDefault column.
  2. Set the default subscription you will work against: az account set --subscription "<subscription-id-or-name>".
  3. Verify: az account show --query "{name:name, id:id}" --output table.

Clean up: nothing was created. If you changed the default subscription, set it back to your usual 1.

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian Health runs patient systems in Azure. Their cloud team creates 3 subscriptions:

  • Meridian-Prod: patient portal, EHR integrations. Only the platform team has Contributor here.
  • Meridian-DevTest: developers experiment freely. Budgets and alerts cap the spend (Day 9).
  • Meridian-Security: log analytics and backup vaults, isolated so a compromised prod admin cannot delete the audit trail.

1 invoice per subscription keeps finance happy, and a developer with Contributor on Meridian-DevTest has zero access to patient data in Meridian-Prod. When the exam asks "how do you isolate billing and access between environments," this is the answer: separate subscriptions.

  1. A company wants each department to receive its own Azure invoice. What is the simplest design?
  2. Put these in order from broadest to narrowest scope: resource group, management group, subscription, resource.
  3. A developer needs Contributor on dev resources but must have no access to production. What do you recommend?
  4. True or false: quotas apply per resource group.

Exam tip

"Billing boundary" is the single most tested subscription idea on AZ-104. If a question mentions invoices, chargeback, or cost separation by department, think subscriptions first, tags second. The second most tested idea is scope: RBAC roles and Azure Policy can be assigned at the management group, subscription, resource group, or resource level, and the exam will ask which scope fits the requirement.

Key takeaways

  • A subscription is the billing boundary and a primary access boundary in Azure.
  • Hierarchy: Entra tenant, management groups, subscriptions, resource groups, resources.
  • Use multiple subscriptions to separate billing, access, and quota ceilings.
  • Know the common offer types: Free Trial, Pay-As-You-Go, EA/MCA, CSP.
  • Quotas apply per subscription per region, not per resource group.
