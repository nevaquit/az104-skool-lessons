The concept

Identity Protection is Microsoft Entra ID's automated security guard for identities. It watches every sign-in and every account for signs of compromise, assigns a risk level, and can respond automatically through policies you define. It requires a Microsoft Entra ID P2 license.

It tracks 2 kinds of risk:

  • Sign-in risk: this particular sign-in looks bad. Examples: sign-in from an anonymous IP address, from an unfamiliar location, or with credentials found in a breach dump.
  • User risk: this account itself is probably compromised, based on patterns across its recent activity.

Each gets a level: none, low, medium, or high. Then 3 policy types automate the response:

  • Sign-in risk policy: for example, medium risk and above must complete multifactor authentication; high risk is blocked or must change password.
  • User risk policy: for example, high-risk users must change their password before they can sign in again.
  • MFA registration policy: prompts users who have not registered for MFA to do it, closing the gap the other 2 policies depend on.

Remediation has 2 sides. Users remediate themselves by changing their password (self-service password reset, Day 28). Administrators work the Risky users and Risky sign-ins blades: investigate the detections behind a flag, then either dismiss the risk (false positive, such as an admin traveling) or confirm the account as compromised or safe, which clears the flag after the user secures the account.

Identity Protection also feeds Conditional Access: sign-in risk and user risk are conditions you can use inside CA policies (Day 31), which is why risk-based access control implies P2.

Key vocabulary

  • Identity Protection: automated detection of and response to identity risk (requires P2).
  • Sign-in risk: the probability that this specific sign-in is not legitimate.
  • User risk: the probability that the account itself is compromised.
  • Risk policy: the automated response to a risk level (require MFA, block, force password change).
  • Risky users / Risky sign-ins: the investigation blades where admins review detections.
  • Remediation: clearing risk by securing the account (password change) or dismissing a false positive.

Try it

Read-only. Empty blades in a fresh tenant are the expected result, not an error.

In the portal:

  1. Microsoft Entra admin center > Protection > Identity Protection > Overview. Note the risk tiles and what each 1 counts.
  2. Open Risky users and Risky sign-ins. If any entries exist, open 1 and read its detections and risk detail to see what the engine actually evaluates.
  3. Open MFA registration policy and see which users it targets and what it requires of them.

Clean up: nothing was changed. As a rule, never dismiss a real risk detection as a drill.

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian Health licensed P2 for all clinical staff and configured 3 policies: the sign-in risk policy requires MFA at medium risk and blocks high-risk sign-ins; the user risk policy forces a password change at high user risk; the MFA registration policy enrolls every new hire in their first week. When a doctor's credentials surfaced in a public breach dump, Identity Protection flagged the user at high risk, the doctor reset the password through SSPR, and the on-call admin reviewed the detections and marked the user safe. Total time from detection to remediation: under an hour, most of it automated.

Exam tip

"Automate the response to risky sign-ins" is the trigger phrase for Identity Protection risk policies, and the P2 license is the most tested fact on this topic. Remember the pairing: Identity Protection detects and scores, Conditional Access enforces. A question that mentions risk as a CA condition is telling you the tenant has P2.

Key takeaways

  • Identity Protection detects sign-in risk and user risk and automates the response (P2 required).
  • 3 policy types: sign-in risk, user risk, and MFA registration.
  • Users remediate with a password change; admins investigate in the Risky users and Risky sign-ins blades.
  • Risk feeds Conditional Access, making access decisions risk-aware.
