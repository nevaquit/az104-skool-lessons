The concept

Microsoft Entra ID is the identity service behind almost everything in Azure. It used to be called Azure Active Directory, and the exam still uses both names, so treat them as the same product. Every sign-in to the Azure portal, every role assignment, and every Conditional Access policy runs through Entra ID.

Think of it as 3 jobs in 1 service:

  • Authentication. Verifying who someone is: passwords, MFA, passwordless sign-in.
  • Authorization identities. RBAC roles (Days 15-20) get assigned to users, groups, and service principals that live in Entra ID. No identity, no access.
  • Single sign-on. 1 identity that opens thousands of SaaS apps: Microsoft 365, Salesforce, ServiceNow, and anything speaking SAML, OAuth 2.0, or OpenID Connect.

1 Entra ID tenant is a dedicated, trusted instance for 1 organization. The trust rule the exam tests: a subscription trusts exactly 1 tenant, while 1 tenant can hold many subscriptions. Users, groups, devices, and apps all live in the tenant.

Editions, and what unlocks what:

  • Free. Comes with every Azure subscription. User and group management, SSO, B2B guests, Security Defaults (Day 30).
  • P1. Adds Conditional Access (Day 31), dynamic groups (Day 27), hybrid identity with Entra Connect (Day 35), and advanced group management.
  • P2. Adds Identity Protection (Day 38), Privileged Identity Management, and Access Reviews.

When a question says "no premium licenses" or "minimize cost," the answer lives in the Free column. When it names Conditional Access, dynamic groups, or Identity Protection, the answer requires P1 or P2.

Key vocabulary

  • Tenant: a dedicated instance of Microsoft Entra ID for 1 organization. Holds users, groups, devices, and apps.
  • Single sign-on (SSO): 1 set of credentials that opens many applications.
  • Identity: the user, group, service principal, or managed identity that RBAC roles get assigned to.
  • Service principal: the identity an application uses to sign in and access resources. Think "user account for apps."
  • Managed identity: a service principal Azure creates and rotates for you, tied to the lifecycle of a resource like a VM. No passwords to store.

Try it

You need any Azure account, including a free 1. Nothing here creates billable resources. Read-only.

In the portal:

  1. Go to the Microsoft Entra admin center (entra.microsoft.com). Under Identity, open Overview. Note your tenant name, tenant ID, and primary domain (something like contoso.onmicrosoft.com).
  2. Open Users > All users. This is the identity store: every sign-in to your Azure resources starts here.
  3. Open Groups > All groups. Compare with Users: identities are the "who," groups are how you manage the "who" at scale.
  4. Open Enterprise applications > All applications. This is SSO in action: every connected SaaS app is registered here.

In the CLI (Cloud Shell is fine):

  1. Show which tenant your subscription trusts: az account show --query "{subscription:name, tenantId:tenantId}" --output table.
  2. List every tenant your account can reach: az account tenant list --output table. Most people see exactly 1.

Clean up: nothing was created or changed.

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian Health has 1 Entra ID tenant, meridianhealth.onmicrosoft.com, holding 3 subscriptions (Meridian-Prod, Meridian-DevTest, Meridian-Security from Day 2). Every doctor, nurse, and billing clerk is a user in that 1 tenant, and SSO means 1 sign-in opens the patient portal app, Microsoft 365, and the Azure portal. When auditors ask "who can touch patient data," the answer starts in Entra ID: the users and groups that the RBAC assignments in each subscription point back to.

Exam tip

The exam constantly tests the license boundary: Free vs P1 vs P2. Memorize the mapping: Conditional Access, dynamic groups, and hybrid identity need P1; Identity Protection, PIM, and Access Reviews need P2. Any question with "no premium licenses" is steering you to Security Defaults or per-user MFA.

Key takeaways

  • Microsoft Entra ID (formerly Azure Active Directory) is the identity plane for Azure: authentication, authorization identities, and SSO.
  • 1 tenant holds many subscriptions; 1 subscription trusts exactly 1 tenant.
  • Free covers users, groups, SSO, B2B, and Security Defaults. P1 adds Conditional Access and dynamic groups. P2 adds Identity Protection, PIM, and Access Reviews.
  • Service principals are identities for apps; managed identities are service principals Azure rotates for you.
