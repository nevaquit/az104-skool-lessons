The concept

Every new tenant gets a starter domain like meridianhealth.onmicrosoft.com, and every user gets a sign-in name to match, such as maya.patel@meridianhealth.onmicrosoft.com. That works, but it rarely matches the company's real email addresses, and users should not have to remember 2 different names.

A custom domain fixes that. You add your real domain, for example meridianhealth.org, prove you own it, and then users can sign in with maya.patel@meridianhealth.org: 1 name for email and sign-in.

The process:

  1. In the Microsoft Entra admin center, go to Identity > Settings > Domain names and select Add custom domain.
  2. Enter the domain name. Entra ID gives you a TXT record to create at your DNS host.
  3. Add that TXT record in your public DNS. This proves ownership.
  4. Back in the portal, select Verify. Entra ID checks DNS for the TXT record.
  5. Optionally set the new domain as primary: new users get it in their sign-in name by default.

Rules the exam tests:

  • You cannot remove the initial onmicrosoft.com domain. It is permanent.
  • You cannot delete a custom domain that is still assigned to any user. Move the users off it first.
  • Adding the domain does not change existing users automatically. Their sign-in names keep the old suffix until you update them (bulk updates were Day 24).
  • A domain is either managed (sign-in handled by Entra ID, used with password hash sync or pass-through authentication) or federated (sign-in handed off to an on-premises federation service such as AD FS).

The exam's favorite question shape: "users must sign in with their email address." The answer is to add and verify a custom domain matching the email domain.

Key vocabulary

  • Custom domain: your organization's real domain added to Entra ID.
  • UPN (User Principal Name): the sign-in name, shaped like an email address.
  • TXT verification record: the DNS record that proves you own the domain.
  • Primary domain: the default suffix for newly created users.
  • Managed domain: authentication handled by Entra ID.
  • Federated domain: authentication handed off to an on-premises federation service.

Try it

Safe on any tenant. You will not complete the wizard, so nothing changes.

In the portal:

  1. Microsoft Entra admin center > Identity > Settings > Domain names. Note the initial onmicrosoft.com domain and which domain is marked primary.
  2. Select + Add custom domain and read the wizard steps up to the DNS verification screen. Stop there. Do not enter a domain you do not own.
  3. Open 1 existing verified domain and look at the DNS records Entra ID asks for: the TXT verification record plus the optional MX and CNAME records for email and Autodiscover.

CLI alternative (read-only):

az rest --method get --url "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/domains" --query "value[].{Domain:id, Verified:isVerified}" --output table

Clean up: nothing was created. Close the wizard without saving.

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian Health's tenant started as meridianhealth.onmicrosoft.com, but staff email is @meridianhealth.org. The admin adds meridianhealth.org as a custom domain, creates the TXT record at the hospital's DNS host, verifies it, and sets it as primary. New hires now get firstname.lastname@meridianhealth.org as both email and sign-in name. The 400 existing onmicrosoft.com sign-in names are updated in bulk over a weekend, and the helpdesk watches for anyone whose saved credentials still use the old suffix.

Exam tip

TXT record verification is the must-know step: no TXT record, no verified domain. Pair it with Day 24: adding the domain never renames existing users by itself. And when a scenario says users sign in with their email address, translate that to "add a custom domain matching the email domain and verify it."

Key takeaways

  • Custom domains let sign-in names match real email addresses.
  • Ownership is proven with a DNS TXT record, then verified in the portal.
  • The initial onmicrosoft.com domain is permanent; a domain in use by users cannot be deleted.
  • Adding a domain does not rename existing users; update their UPNs separately.
  • Domains are managed (Entra ID authenticates) or federated (on-premises service authenticates).
