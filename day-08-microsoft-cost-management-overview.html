The concept

Microsoft Cost Management is where Azure spending becomes visible and controllable. It answers 3 questions: what did we spend, what will we spend, and how do we spend less. (Billing, the invoicing side, lives next to it under Cost Management + Billing.)

What you can do with it:

  • Cost analysis. Slice accumulated or daily costs by resource, resource group, tag, meter, or location. Grouping by the Department tag from Day 7 is how chargeback actually happens.
  • Budgets and alerts. Covered in depth on Day 9: set spending thresholds and get notified.
  • Cost alerts. Built-in anomaly and threshold alerts under Cost Management.
  • Invoices and payment. View and download invoices, manage payment methods, at the billing account scope.
  • Price calculator. Estimate costs before you deploy anything. The exam treats "estimate first" as best practice.

The scope hierarchy matters: billing account at the top, then billing profile, invoice section, subscription, and resource group. Your view of costs depends on which scope you open.

Access is role based, and the exam tests least privilege here: Cost Management Reader lets finance see every dollar without touching anything. Cost Management Contributor can also manage budgets and configuration. Never hand finance Contributor on the subscription just so they can see the bill.

2 ways to pay less that the exam mentions by name: reservations (commit to 1 or 3 years of a VM or database for a steep discount) and savings plans (commit to an hourly spend amount instead of a specific resource). Both are prepaid commitments: deleting the resource does not refund them.

Key vocabulary

  • Cost Management: the Azure toolset for analyzing, budgeting, and alerting on cloud spend.
  • Billing scope: the level costs roll up to: billing account, billing profile, invoice section, subscription, resource group.
  • Cost analysis: the view that breaks costs down by resource, tag, meter, or time.
  • Chargeback: billing internal departments for their cloud usage, usually via tags.
  • Reservation / savings plan: 1 or 3 year prepaid commitments that discount compute costs.

Try it

Read only. Nothing is created and nothing is billed.

In the portal:

  1. Search for Cost Management and open it. Set the scope to your subscription.
  2. Open Cost analysis. Change the view to Accumulated costs and set Group by to Resource. This is your spend broken down resource by resource.
  3. Change Group by to Tag. Notice which costs appear as untagged: those are the resources your Day 7 tagging strategy would catch.
  4. In the left menu, open Cost alerts and review the built-in alert rules. You will build your own on Day 9.

The command line is not the natural tool for exploring cost data, so this lab stays in the portal. Budgets get CLI treatment on Day 9.

Clean up: nothing was created.

Worked example: Meridian Health

On the first Monday of each month, Meridian's cloud team opens Cost analysis grouped by the Department tag. Last month: cardiology 42 percent, radiology 31 percent, dev and test 27 percent. 2 surprises surface: an idle VM in dev still burning 180 dollars a month, and 3 unattached disks nobody owns. The team tags the disks Owner: unknown, gives the owner 2 weeks to claim them, then deletes them. Monthly review, tag driven, no drama.

Exam tip

Least privilege shows up here as Cost Management Reader for finance teams. If an answer choice gives finance Contributor on the subscription "so they can see costs," it is wrong: Reader at the right scope does the job. Also remember the scope hierarchy: your cost view changes completely depending on whether you open a billing account, a subscription, or a resource group.

Key takeaways

  • Cost Management answers what you spent, what you will spend, and how to spend less.
  • Slice costs by resource, resource group, tag, meter, or time in Cost analysis.
  • Finance gets Cost Management Reader, not Contributor on the subscription.
  • Estimate with the price calculator before you deploy.
  • Reservations and savings plans trade commitment for discount and are not refunded if you delete the resource.
