The concept

Azure ships more than 100 built-in roles, but 4 of them carry most of the exam weight:

  • Owner: full access to everything, including managing access for others. Can assign roles and manage locks.
  • Contributor: full access to manage resources, except managing access. Cannot assign roles to others and cannot manage locks.
  • Reader: view everything, change nothing. Cannot view secrets; for example, cannot read storage account keys.
  • User Access Administrator: manage access only. Can assign roles but cannot manage resources.

The Contributor versus Owner distinction is the single most tested role fact: the only practical difference is the authorization permission. If a scenario says "must be able to grant access to others," Contributor is wrong and Owner (or User Access Administrator) is right.

Then comes the plane distinction:

  • Management plane: managing the resource itself: creating it, changing settings, reading keys. Contributor operates here.
  • Data plane: working with the data inside: reading blobs, querying tables, accessing files. This needs DataActions, which Contributor does not have. A Contributor who opens a storage account in the portal and tries to browse blobs gets an authorization error. Data access needs data roles such as Storage Blob Data Contributor or Storage Blob Data Reader.

Beyond the big 4, learn the pattern, not the list: service-specific roles like Virtual Machine Contributor or Website Contributor narrow the blast radius. Always pick the narrowest built-in that fits before building a custom role (Day 18).

Key vocabulary

  • Management plane: operations on the resource itself, covered by Actions.
  • Data plane: operations on the data inside the resource, covered by DataActions.
  • DataActions: the permission set for data access, held by data roles, not by Contributor.
  • Least privilege: granting only the permissions the job needs, nothing more.

Try it

You need any Azure account, including a free 1. This lab is read only. Nothing is created or changed.

In the portal:

  1. Open any scope, then Access control (IAM), then the Roles tab.
  2. Open Owner, Contributor, and Reader 1 at a time and view each permissions JSON. Compare them, especially the NotActions that carve Contributor down from Owner.
  3. Search for "Storage Blob Data" and open Storage Blob Data Contributor. Notice it grants DataActions instead of Actions: this role reads blobs but cannot manage the storage account.

In the CLI (Cloud Shell is fine):

  1. Compare the 3 core roles: az role definition list --query "[?roleName=='Owner' || roleName=='Contributor' || roleName=='Reader'].{Role:roleName, Description:description}" --output table

Worked example: Meridian Health

Meridian assigns the big 4 with intent:

  • 2 senior admins: Owner at the management group, eligible through PIM rather than permanent.
  • Platform team: Contributor on Meridian-Prod. They manage everything in production but cannot grant access, so access reviews stay with the senior admins.
  • Auditors: Reader on both subscriptions. Full visibility, zero change ability.
  • Analytics team: Storage Blob Data Reader on the data lake storage account. They can read the research blobs but cannot change the account, rotate keys, or see anything outside that account.

The analytics assignment is the lesson: the team needed data access, not resource management, so a data role was the least-privilege answer.

Check your understanding

  1. Which roles can assign access to other users, and what permission makes that possible?
  2. A Contributor opens a storage account and cannot browse its blobs. Why?
  3. What is the narrowest built-in role for someone who only needs to view dashboards and costs?
  4. True or false: Reader can restart a virtual machine.

Exam tip

"Manage access" or "grant permissions to others" means Owner or User Access Administrator, never Contributor. Data access questions always hinge on DataActions: if the scenario involves reading blobs, files, or tables, reach for a data role. When in doubt between 2 roles, the exam rewards the narrower 1.

Key takeaways

  • Owner manages everything including access; Contributor manages everything except access; Reader views only.
  • Only Owner and User Access Administrator can assign roles and manage locks.
  • Contributor covers the management plane; data access needs DataActions roles like Storage Blob Data Contributor.
  • Pick the narrowest built-in role that fits before creating a custom role.
  • Least privilege is the lens for every role question on the exam.
